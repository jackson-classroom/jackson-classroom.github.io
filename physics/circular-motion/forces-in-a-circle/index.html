Find the center. Then add it up.

The net force in a circle points toward the center, and it equals m · v² / r. To solve a problem, figure out which real forces point toward the center, which point away, and add them up.

One method for every circle

On page 1 you found that the net force in a circle points toward the center, and that Fnet = m · ac. Since ac = v² / r, you can put them together:

Newton's second law, in a circleAdd up the forces along the line to the center. Forces toward the center count as positive. Forces away from the center count as negative.
(toward the center) − (away from the center) = m · v² / r
  1. Draw the free-body diagram. Only real forces: Fgrav, Fnorm, Ffrict, Ftens.Never draw an extra "centripetal force" arrow. The net force is what the real forces add up to.
  2. Find the center. Which way is the center of the circle from where the object is right now?
  3. Sort the forces. Which ones point toward the center? Which point away?Forces at a right angle to that line (like up and down on a flat curve) don't go in this equation. They cancel each other.
  4. Write the equation: (toward) − (away) = m · v² / r.
  5. Plug in and solve for whatever the problem asks for.

You'll need two situations: a car on a flat curve, and an object at the top or bottom of a vertical loop.

Flat curve: friction is the net force

A car turns on a flat road. Look at it from behind. Gravity pulls it down, the road pushes it up, and the road's friction on the tires pushes it sideways, toward the center of the curve.

  • Up and down cancel. The car isn't going up or down, so Fnorm = Fgrav = m · g.
  • Friction is the only force toward the center. So friction is the net force.
Fnorm = m · g

Up and down cancel

On flat ground, the normal force equals the weight.

Ffrict = m · v² / r

Friction is Fnet

The friction needed to make the car turn.

Ffrict = μ · Fnorm

The most the road can give

From Unit 3. If the curve needs more than this, the car slides.

Worked example: find μ

A 1500 kg car takes a flat curve with a radius of 60 m at 18 m/s. Find the smallest μ that keeps it from sliding.

Ffrict = m · v² / r
Ffrict = 1500 kg × (18 m/s)² / 60 m = 8100 N
Fnorm = m · g = 1500 kg × 9.8 m/s² = 14700 N
μ = Ffrict / Fnorm = 8100 N / 14700 N = 0.55

The tires and road need a coefficient of friction of at least 0.55. Less than that, and the road can't grip hard enough.

Worked example: find the fastest speed

A 1200 kg car drives on a wet, flat curve with a radius of 40 m. The coefficient of friction is 0.5. Find the fastest speed v it can have without sliding.

Fnorm = m · g = 1200 kg × 9.8 m/s² = 11760 N
Ffrict = μ · Fnorm = 0.5 × 11760 N = 5880 N
Ffrict = m · v² / r
5880 N = 1200 kg × v² / 40 m
v² = 5880 × 40 / 1200 = 196
v = √196 = 14 m/s

Any faster, and the curve needs more friction than the road can give. The car can't turn tightly enough and slides toward the outside of the curve.

Solving for v: multiply both sides by r, divide by m, then take the square root. Don't stop at v². That's the most common mistake on this kind of problem.

The top and the bottom of a loop

Now stand the circle up: a ball swung on a string in a vertical circle, or a roller coaster going through a loop. Gravity points along the line to the center now, so it goes in the equation.

The tricky part: gravity always points down, but the center isn't always below you. At the top of the loop, the center is below, so gravity points toward it. At the bottom, the center is above, so gravity points away from it.

Ftens + Fgrav = m · v² / r

At the top

The string pulls down toward the center. Gravity pulls down too, also toward the center. They work together.

Ftens − Fgrav = m · v² / r

At the bottom

The string pulls up toward the center. Gravity pulls down, away from the center. It works against the tension.

Worked example: at the top

A 0.5 kg ball on a 0.8 m string is swung in a vertical circle. At the top it's moving at 5 m/s. Find the tension Ftens.

Toward the centerAway
Ftens, Fgravnothing
Fgrav = m · g = 0.5 kg × 9.8 m/s² = 4.9 N
m · v² / r = 0.5 kg × (5 m/s)² / 0.8 m = 15.63 N
Ftens + Fgrav = m · v² / r
Ftens + 4.9 N = 15.63 N
Ftens = 10.73 N

Gravity does 4.9 N of the job, so the string only has to add the rest.

Worked example: at the bottom

At the bottom, the same ball is moving at 6 m/s. Find the tension Ftens.

Toward the centerAway
FtensFgrav
Fgrav = 4.9 N
m · v² / r = 0.5 kg × (6 m/s)² / 0.8 m = 22.5 N
Ftens − Fgrav = m · v² / r
Ftens − 4.9 N = 22.5 N
Ftens = 27.4 N

The string has to cancel gravity and supply the 22.5 N net force. That's why a string is most likely to break at the bottom.

Gravity doesn't flip. The center does. Fgrav points straight down at the top and at the bottom. What changes is whether "down" is toward the center (top, so add it) or away from the center (bottom, so subtract it).

Roller coasters: the track pushes instead

A roller coaster in a loop works the same way. The only change is that the track pushes on the cart (a normal force) instead of a string pulling. The track always pushes from the outside of the loop, toward the center.

WhereToward the centerAwayEquation
Ball on a string, topFtens Fgrav—Ftens + Fgrav = m · v² / r
Ball on a string, bottomFtensFgravFtens − Fgrav = m · v² / r
Coaster, top of the loop (upside down)Fnorm Fgrav—Fnorm + Fgrav = m · v² / r
Coaster, bottom of the loopFnormFgravFnorm − Fgrav = m · v² / r

Worked example

A 600 kg cart goes through a loop with a radius of 10 m. It moves at 12 m/s at the top and 20 m/s at the bottom. Find the normal force Fnorm at the top and at the bottom.

Fgrav = 600 kg × 9.8 m/s² = 5880 N
Top: m · v² / r = 600 × 12² / 10 = 8640 N
Fnorm + 5880 N = 8640 N
Fnorm = 2760 N (down)
Bottom: m · v² / r = 600 × 20² / 10 = 24000 N
Fnorm − 5880 N = 24000 N
Fnorm = 29880 N (up)

At the bottom, the track pushes about 5 times as hard as the cart's weight. Your seat does the same thing to you, which is why you feel squashed into it at the bottom of a loop and nearly weightless at the top.

Watch-outs

  • Gravity always gets subtracted.Only when it points away from the center, at the bottom. At the top it points toward the center, so it gets added.
  • At the top of the loop, the string (or the track) pushes up to hold the object up.A string can only pull toward the center, which at the top is down. The track is above the upside-down cart, so it pushes down too.
  • The tension is m · v² / r.The net force is m · v² / r. In a vertical loop, gravity is part of the net force, so the tension is m · v² / r minus Fgrav at the top, plus Fgrav at the bottom.
  • The normal force on a coaster cart always equals its weight.Only when it isn't accelerating up or down. At the bottom of a loop, the track pushes harder than the weight.
  • On a flat curve, the normal force pulls the car toward the center.The normal force points straight up. The only sideways force is friction, so friction is the net force.
  • You found v², so you're done.Take the square root. A car going 196 m/s around a curve would be going over 400 mph.
