Quick reference

Everything on this card showed up in the first three days of class.

Position x
Where she is, measured from an agreed zero — for us, the motion detector. It's a location, not a trip.
Distance d
The length of the whole path walked. Always positive, always adds up. Never negative.
Displacement Δx
Δx = xfinal − xinitial. Start to finish, straight, with a sign. It can be negative, and it can be zero on a walk of 20 m if she returns to where she started.
Speed vs. velocity
Speed is how fast (no sign). Velocity is how fast and which way (has a sign). Two students can walk at the same speed with opposite velocities.
Slope of a position–time graph
slope = rise / run = Δx / Δt = velocity. Pick two points you can read exactly off the gridlines, then subtract — final minus initial, in both directions.
What the shape is telling you
Flat line → at rest.
Rising line → moving away from the detector (positive velocity).
Falling line → moving back toward the detector (negative velocity).
Steeper line → faster. Two lines with the same steepness have the same speed even if one rises and one falls.
A corner (a kink) → the velocity changed. A peak or valley → she changed direction.
The trap to avoid
A position–time graph is not a picture of the hallway. A rising line is not a hill, and a line that goes up then down is not a trip over a bump — it's a walk out and back. Press play on any graph in here if that ever stops feeling obvious.
Units, every time
Position and distance in m, time in s, velocity and speed in m/s. An answer without a unit is an incomplete answer.
