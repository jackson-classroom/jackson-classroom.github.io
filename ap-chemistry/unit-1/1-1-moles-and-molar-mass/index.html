Moles and Molar Mass

Atoms are far too small to count one at a time, so chemists count them by the mole. Molar mass is the bridge between the grams you can weigh on a balance and the number of particles that actually react.

Q1Why count by the mole?One mole is 6.022 × 1023 particles, the number that turns atomic masses into grams
Q2How do grams become moles?Molar mass in g/mol from the periodic table, n = m/M
Q3How many particles is that?Chaining grams, moles, and particles together with dimensional analysis
