What a consistent diagram shows

4.3.A.1
Equations and particles

A balanced chemical equation, in any of its forms, can be translated into a particle representation, and a particle representation can be translated back into an equation.

Hydrogen burns in oxygen: . Here is a container that starts with four H2 molecules and three O2 molecules, before and after the reaction.

The four H2 react with two of the O2. The third O2 has nothing left to react with, so it’s still there afterward. Count the atoms: 8 H and 6 O in both boxes.

Every diagram like this has to get four things right.

1 · Atoms

Every atom is conserved

Count each element in both boxes. The numbers must match.

2 · Formulas

Every particle matches its formula

H2 is a pair of atoms, NH3 is one N with three H, and NO3− is one unit.

3 · Ratios

What reacts matches the coefficients

H2 and O2 react 2 : 1 above, and H2O forms in the same amount as H2 used.

4 · Leftovers and states

What doesn’t react stays

Unreacted particles stay in the after box, unchanged. Gases spread out, liquids crowd together, and solids pack into a lattice.

4.3.A.1

From a picture back to an equation

Nitrogen and hydrogen react to form ammonia. Write the equation using only the diagram.

  1. Count each particle in both boxes. Before: 3 N2, 6 H2. After: 4 NH3, 1 N2.
  2. Set aside what didn’t change. One N2 is in both boxes, so it never reacted.
  3. What disappeared is a reactant; what appeared is a product. 2 N2 and 6 H2 were used up and 4 NH3 formed: .

Leftovers aren’t products. Writing technically balances, but the N2 on the right never reacted. An equation shows only what changed.
4.3.A.1

Ionic compounds in solution

Diagrams of reactions in water follow everything from Topic 4.2. Here, silver nitrate and sodium chloride solutions are mixed. Water molecules aren’t drawn, which is common in AP diagrams.

Net ionic equation:

  • Dissolved ionic compounds are separate ions. There are no AgNO3 or NaCl “molecules” in the solution.
  • The precipitate is a packed solid, with Ag+ and Cl− alternating in a lattice.
  • Spectators are still there. Na+ and NO3− are in the after box, dissolved and unchanged.
4.3.A

Physical processes

The learning objective includes physical processes too. In a physical change, the particles themselves don’t change. Only their arrangement does.

Water boiling

: the same eight molecules, close together in the liquid and spread out in the gas.

Sodium chloride dissolving

: the lattice comes apart into separate ions, with water molecules not drawn.

Watch out

Diagrams that lose the point

Each of these breaks one of the four rules.

Ionic compounds drawn as pairs

NaCl(aq) is separate Na+ and Cl− ions. Drawing them stuck together shows a solid that didn’t dissolve.

Leftover reactant left out

The mixture started with 2 N2 and 3 H2. Only one N2 can react; without the other, 2 N atoms vanish.

Polyatomic ions broken apart

KNO3(aq) contains K+ and NO3−. The nitrate ion stays one unit; it doesn’t fall apart into N and O atoms.

Diatomic elements as single atoms

Oxygen gas is O2. Six separate O atoms is a different substance from three O2 molecules.
